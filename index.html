Conventional ponds

Lower initial investment and simpler operation. Larger land demand, odour and overflow risks, and uncontrolled methane can remain.

Question:

How would you demonstrate adequate treatment and manage remaining emissions?

UASB + biogas

Compact anaerobic treatment with gas recovery and aerobic post-treatment. Requires suitable pretreatment, stable loading and trained operators.

Question:

What happens if gas capture or the biological process performs poorly?

Integrated recovery

Adds water polishing and planned sludge recovery. Requires investment, energy, maintenance and quality checks for each recovered product.

Question:

Are there practical users for the recovered water, energy and nutrients?

Facilitator & learner guide

Learn by testing.
Decide with evidence.

Learning outcome

By the end of the lesson, students can explain why untreated POME harms a river, describe how three ways of treating it work (open ponds, a biogas digester with aeration, and full resource recovery) and choose one for a Malaysian mill with reasons.

Success looks like: a choice backed by two reasons, one problem it still has and one thing the mill must do first.

Target learners

  • Who: secondary school (SMK) students in Form 2, around 14 years old. They know that living things need oxygen and that water can be polluted, but have not met BOD, biogas or treatment plants.
  • Curriculum link: KSSM Science topics on water, water pollution and ecosystems (teachers should match the exact chapter in their current scheme of work).
  • Setting: one laptop or tablet per pair in the computer lab or classroom, any modern browser; the graphics adapt to slower school computers.
  • Role of the teacher: sets the scene, prompts pairs during the games and leads the final discussion.

Key words

POME
Palm oil mill effluent: the hot, dirty wastewater from a palm oil mill.
BOD
How much oxygen bacteria would use to break down the waste. High BOD = less oxygen for fish.
COD
A quick lab measure of how much pollution in the water can be oxidised.
Anaerobic
Without air. Anaerobic bacteria in a digester make biogas.
Aerobic
With air. Aerobic bacteria use oxygen to finish cleaning the water.
Biogas
A gas made mostly of methane that can be burnt to make electricity.
Methane (CH₄)
A greenhouse gas much stronger than carbon dioxide; open ponds let it escape.
Sludge
The settled solids left after treatment; it can be composted.

Why digital, and what stays offline

What the digital tool adds

  • Learning by doing: students change one thing and see all three pathways respond at once, instead of copying a table from the board.
  • Seeing hidden processes: granules, gas bubbles, molecules and pipe flows that cannot be observed in a lecture or on a site visit.
  • A safe site visit: mills are far from most schools and digesters involve dangerous gas; students can walk the plant and talk to workers without risk.
  • Games that teach: five different Mill Arcade games (storm rescue, building the plant, running the control room, managing a budget, a quiz race) keep 14-year-olds active and motivated.
  • Immediate feedback and a record: trail quizzes, the reasoning-structure check and an exportable learning record for assessment.

What we keep non-digital on purpose

  • Pair discussion of the choice, because explaining a decision to a friend is the core skill.
  • Facilitator debrief on where the model is simplified and why real data are needed.
  • Fallback: if devices are limited, the poster, pathway cards and mill brief can be printed and the numbers worked by hand.
  • Limits of the tool: needs a browser with WebGL; the model is illustrative, not a design or compliance calculation.

Model transparency & evidence

What the numbers mean

All defaults are illustrative teaching assumptions, not measurements. Organic load = flow × influent COD ÷ 1,000. Fixed total COD removal fractions are 90%, 95% and 98% for the three pathways. Residual COD = influent COD × (1 − removal). These chosen fractions illustrate comparisons and do not establish that one technology always performs better.

Illustrative methane = influent COD load × 70% anaerobic removal × 0.25 m³/kg COD removed × capture fraction, for recovery pathways only. Pond methane capture is zero; zero capture does not mean zero methane generation. Water substitution = flow × reuse fraction for integrated recovery only; losses and water-quality limitations are not modelled.

What this model cannot tell you

No BOD prediction, legal compliance determination, engineering sizing, financial payback or net greenhouse-gas assessment. It does not simulate biological kinetics, nutrient removal, gas purity, methane leakage after collection, parasitic energy, sludge quality or actual hydraulic losses. Capture and reuse sliders are independent assumptions, not operational guarantees.

Connection to your Part A

The three pathways, the five-stage integrated design and the trade-offs come from the group's Part A report, "Conventional Pollutant Removal versus Integrated Pollution Control and Resource Recovery System for Palm Oil Mill Effluent", and its poster "POME in Malaysia: from wastewater problem to renewable resource". This prototype turns that comparison into an active decision exercise. Assessing learners

Check each pair's decision for four things: a correct explanation of how their choice cleans POME, two reasons, one problem it still has, and one thing the mill must do first. The trail quizzes, the arcade games and the final quiz check key ideas; the written decision checks whether students can apply them.

Demonstration script (about 7 minutes)

  1. 0:00 · Outcome and learners. State the learning outcome and that it is for Form 2 SMK students. Show the Start here poster and the big question.
  2. 0:45 · Investigate. Click one conventional problem and one recovery stage; make a prediction.
  3. 1:45 · Explore UASB in 3D. Press Play as a worker, walk to one trail stop, answer its quiz and ask a worker a question.
  4. 3:15 · Switch to Integrated recovery. Show the new units appear; click UF/RO and the biogas holder for their explanations.
  5. 4:15 · Experiment and play. Change methane capture and save a run, then open the Mill Arcade and show one game (Build the Treatment Train or Biogas Mission).
  6. 5:30 · Decide. Choose a pathway for the mill brief, show the prediction recall and the reasoning check, then the quiz feedback.
  7. 6:30 · Rationale. Explain why digital was chosen, what stays offline, and the model's limits. Export the learning record.
